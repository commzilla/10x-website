Prop Firm Statistics 2027: Pass Rates, Payouts, Rules and Spend

By the 10X Trader editorial team Updated 27 November 2026 20 min read

Most prop firm traders never get paid. In the largest data set we found, covering about 100,000 traders at 10 firms, 14% passed a challenge and about 7% ever received a payout (FPFX Tech data reported by Finance Magnates, 18 September 2024). This page adds two counts of our own. The 2027 10X Prop Firm Rules Index sets out the rules and prices at 24 forex and CFD prop firms that accept UK traders, read from each firm's own pages on 27 November 2026. The 2027 10X Prop Trader Community Study counts what traders report in 566 public posts from 2026: why payouts were denied, what ended accounts, how long payouts took and how much people say they've spent.

Key takeaways

  • In FPFX Tech's data on about 100,000 traders, about 7% ever received a payout (Finance Magnates, 2024). We found no independent, audited pass rate for the industry.
  • The 2027 10X Prop Firm Rules Index found that 23 of 24 firms describe the accounts they give traders as simulated, demo or virtual.
  • The 2027 10X Prop Firm Rules Index found a median list price of $549 for a $100,000 2-step challenge (17 firms priced in US dollars, 27 November 2026). Instant accounts cost a median $1,142 at a mix of account sizes, and 3.6 times as much for each $1,000 you're allowed to lose.
  • In the 2027 10X Prop Trader Community Study (566 posts, 2 January to 26 November 2026), the most common reason given for a denied payout was copy trading, account sharing or a shared IP or device (31 of 94 posts). Consistency rules cost traders payouts too: 2 posts said the firm refused a payout over one, and 6 more said one held a payout back.
  • In the same study, the 75 people who said what they'd spent on prop firms put it at $5,279 on average, leaving out the five biggest figures (over $40,000), and half had spent $2,000 or less.

Researched and published by 10X Trader, which offers the 10X broker account. Leverage amplifies losses as well as gains. The most you can lose is your deposit. This page is general information, not financial advice.

Terms used on this page

  • 2-step challenge: a paid test in two phases. You hit a profit target in each phase without breaking the loss limits, then get a "funded" account.
  • Instant account: a funded account sold with no test.
  • Maximum loss: the most the account can fall before it's closed. Static means it's measured from your starting balance and doesn't move. Trailing means it follows your highest balance up.
  • Loss allowance: that maximum loss in dollars. The fee per $1,000 of loss allowance is what you pay for each $1,000 you're allowed to lose.
  • Consistency rule: a cap on how much of your profit can come from one day (for example, no single day above 15% of the total).

How many traders pass a prop firm challenge and get paid?

About 1 in 7 traders passed a challenge and about 1 in 14 was ever paid, in the largest data set we found. That data comes from FPFX Tech, a company that sells software to prop firms. It covered more than 300,000 accounts held by about 100,000 traders at 10 firms. 14% passed and got a funded account, about 45% of those received a payout (7% of all traders), and the average payout was 4% of the account size (Finance Magnates, 18 September 2024). The figures are the supplier's and haven't been audited.

We found two firms' own figures, and neither is checked by anyone else:

  • The Funded Trader: its chief executive said its pass rate was 5% to 10%, and about 20% of those who passed received payouts (Finance Magnates, 18 March 2025). That works out at about 1% to 2% of its clients. The same report says 1,272 people had been waiting more than a year for money the firm owed them.
  • Hola Prime: its website footer says "the customer pass rate was 25.07% for the Forex Challenge/Evaluation program" between 15 October 2025 and 30 September 2026 (Hola Prime, checked 27 November 2026). That's the firm's own claim, and its definition of who is counted isn't clear.

We found no independent, audited pass rate for the industry. Treat any single "pass rate" you see as one firm's or one supplier's number.

UK context: how many CFD traders lose money?

The FCA says about 80% of customers lose money when investing in CFDs. That figure comes from its 2022 findings, quoted in its December 2025 Discussion Paper DP25/3 (para 1.23). Since 2019, UK CFD providers have had to show potential customers the percentage of the firm's retail client accounts that make losses (FCA, PS19/18, 1 July 2019). That's why UK CFD providers show the share of their retail accounts that lose money.

Prop firm accounts work differently, and the figures above don't carry across:

  • The account is usually simulated. The 2027 10X Prop Firm Rules Index found that 23 of the 24 firms describe the accounts they give traders as simulated, demo or virtual on their own pages. FTMO, for example, says "all accounts we provide to our clients are demo accounts with fictitious funds". FXIFY was the one exception: it says it provides a live account once you pass its assessment. Two firms need a note: Audacity Capital says its accounts "primarily" operate in a simulated environment, and City Traders Imperium's help centre calls two of its products "real funding", although its legal footer says all trading is in a demo environment.
  • The money at risk is the fee. You pay a fee for each attempt. The firms say the trading happens in a simulated environment, and any payout is a reward the firm pays you.
  • Check who you're dealing with. The FCA Firm Checker and Warning List show whether a firm is authorised and which firms the FCA is concerned about. The FCA's warnings spell out what dealing with an unauthorised firm means: no access to the Financial Ombudsman Service and no FSCS protection (for example, an FCA warning of 12 August 2025 about an unauthorised website).

For how the fee model works, see how prop firms make money. For the other ways to trade a bigger account than your deposit, see funded trading accounts.

The 2027 10X Prop Trader Community Study: what traders report

We found no public data set that counts why payouts are denied, what ends accounts or how long payouts take, so we read public posts and counted them.

Method

We read 1,337 public posts and reviews from 2026, collected on 27 November 2026. The 566 we kept are dated 2 January to 26 November 2026. The posts are from anywhere in the world, not only the UK:

  • Reddit posts from 84 searches, and the replies under 29 question threads;
  • posts on X from 15 searches, and the replies under 9 question threads;
  • the newest 1- and 2-star Trustpilot reviews for 12 of the firms in the Rules Index below, which reach back only to 20 October 2026;
  • 14 Quora answers, none of which we used (9 were promotional and 5 had no first-hand detail).

What counted as a post: one Reddit post or top-level reply, one X post or reply, or one Trustpilot review.

What we kept: a post was included if it described the writer's own experience with a forex, CFD or general prop firm. That left 566 posts. We left out:

  • 407 with no first-hand detail;
  • 154 promotional posts (discount codes, firm accounts, sellers);
  • 142 about futures-only firms;
  • 9 duplicates, and 59 for other reasons.

How posts were coded: each one on six questions, plus money spent. Every code carries a short quote as evidence, kept internally.

Reading the figures: they count what people chose to write, so they aren't rates for all traders. People with a bad experience post more. Trustpilot reviews were picked from the negative pages, so they're shown separately where that matters. The writers' claims weren't checked with the firms.

Why prop firms deny payouts

94 of the 566 posts we analysed (2 January to 26 November 2026) reported a denied payout. The reason the firm gave, as the writer reported it, was most often copy trading, account sharing or a shared IP or device: 31 of the 94. That was 18 of 64 on Reddit and X and 13 of 30 on Trustpilot.

What ended accounts

234 of the 566 posts (2 January to 26 November 2026) described an account that ended. 131 named no rule; many said only that they had blown the account. Of the 103 that named one, 84 named a rule other than the loss limits, such as copy trading, shared IP or devices, one-sided trading, news or lot-size rules. Daily loss was named in 13, maximum loss in 6, and a consistency rule in none. The negative Trustpilot reviews lean towards firm-side closures: 44 of the 84 "other rule" posts are Trustpilot reviews, against 40 from Reddit and X.

How long prop firm payouts take

26 of the 566 posts (2 January to 26 November 2026) reported a late or missing payout: 17 of 459 Reddit and X posts and replies, and 9 of 107 Trustpilot reviews. Among the 14 that said how long they'd waited, the median wait was 14 days (range 5 to 60). When they posted, 15 were still waiting, 6 said they had never been paid and 3 had been paid late.

How many accounts traders run at once

Among the 20 traders in the study who said how many prop accounts they run at the same time (21 posts), the median was 2 (range 1 to 40). FPFX Tech's data counts firms rather than accounts: it found traders used 2.2 prop firms on average (Finance Magnates, 18 September 2024).

How much traders spend on prop firms

In the 2027 10X Prop Trader Community Study (566 posts, 2 January to 26 November 2026), the 75 people who said how much they had spent on prop firms put it at $5,279 on average, leaving out the five biggest figures (over $40,000), and half had spent $2,000 or less. The middle half spent between $500 and $6,000. With the five biggest included ($45,000 to $119,333), the 80 people averaged $10,072, with a middle figure of $2,400.

How these figures were counted:

  • Each person counts once, using their latest post.
  • A figure counts if it covers what they'd spent in total, so far this year, or with no period given.
  • If someone only said how much they were down, that figure is used. It can only understate what they spent.
  • Figures for a single month and the fee for one account are left out.
  • Figures written with a $ sign count as US dollars. 32 of the 75 had no currency sign ("9k") and are counted as dollars too, because most of the firms price in dollars.
  • Figures in pounds or euros are left out, and nothing is converted.

The closest outside figure is a poll by PipFarm, a prop firm, of 459 of its own clients. It found the average trader had invested $4,270 in prop firm challenges, and almost 60% had lost money overall (Finance Magnates, 13 August 2024). FPFX Tech's data put spend at about $800 per account, typically across three challenges.

Moving to another firm

29 of the 566 posts (2 January to 26 November 2026) mention moving: 12 had moved to another firm, 4 to their own or a broker account, and 13 were considering it. The most common reason given was a denied or cut payout (8 of 29). The sample is small, and we'll count it again in January.

Prop firm comparison: $100k 2-step rules and prices at 21 firms

The 2027 10X Prop Firm Rules Index found that the $100,000 2-step challenge looks very alike from firm to firm. 21 of the 24 firms sell one, and 20 of those 21 use a static maximum loss, measured from your starting balance. Only AquaFunded's is trailing. Just 4 of the 21 add a consistency rule on the default set-up.

Method

We picked 25 forex and CFD prop firms that accept UK traders, by how visible they were in Google UK results and on PropFirmMatch's UK list (an affiliate site, used only to build the list).

  • Reading the pages: we read each firm's own pricing, rules and help pages on 27 November 2026. Every figure links to its page.
  • Products compared: each firm's main $100,000 2-step challenge, and an instant account where it sells one.
  • Firms left out: DNA Funded's site was down all day, so 24 firms are in. Of those, 21 sell a $100k 2-step; E8 Markets and Lark Funding don't sell one, and FTUK stopped selling its 2-step on 14 September 2026.
  • Prices: the price shown before any discount code. Black Friday codes were running at most firms.
  • Currencies: FTMO, BrightFunded and The Trading Pit price in euros. They're shown in euros and kept out of the US-dollar medians.
  • Fee per $1,000 of loss allowance: the fee divided by the most you can lose before the account is closed, in thousands of dollars.

Prop firms with no consistency rule

16 of the 21 firms selling a $100,000 2-step state no consistency rule on the default set-up (27 November 2026): Alpha Capital Group, Audacity Capital, Blueberry Funded, BrightFunded, City Traders Imperium, Finotive Funding, Fintokei, For Traders, FTMO, FundedNext, FundingPips, Goat Funded Trader, Hola Prime, Maven Trading, The Trading Pit and Top One Trader. Instant funding prop firms compared: prices, drawdown and consistency rules

Instant accounts skip the challenge. The 2027 10X Prop Firm Rules Index found they cost more, and most use a trailing loss limit and a consistency rule. Of the 21 firms that sell one, 17 use a trailing maximum loss, which follows your highest balance up. 14 add a consistency rule that caps how much of your profit can come from one day. For each $1,000 you're allowed to lose, an instant account cost a median $199.83, against $55 on a 2-step.

What it takes to earn back what traders spend

Challenge profit isn't paid out, so apart from fee refunds, the money comes back only from profit you withdraw from a funded account, after the firm's share. At the 80% split most firms start you on, a trader needs $1.25 of profit for every $1 they've spent. Some details matter when you compare firms:

  • Bigger accounts are rarer. Only 8 of the 21 firms sell a $200,000 2-step.

What the numbers can't tell you

Both studies have limits, and we'd rather spell them out:

  • The Community Study counts posts, not traders. People who had a bad experience post more, and people who were paid are the most likely to post what they spent next to what they made. The posts come from anywhere in the world, and one person can appear on both Reddit and X. The figures describe what was written, not what all traders experience.
  • Outside pass rates aren't audited. FPFX Tech's data comes from a supplier, The Funded Trader's from its own chief executive, and PipFarm's from a poll of its own clients.
  • Rules and prices change. Every row is dated 27 November 2026, during Black Friday, when most firms ran discount codes. Check the firm's own page before you buy.
  • This page states facts, not recommendations. It doesn't say which firm is best, or whether a prop firm suits you.

Prop firm statistics FAQ

What percentage of traders pass a prop firm challenge?

We found no independent, audited figure. In FPFX Tech's data on about 100,000 traders at 10 firms, 14% passed a challenge and got a funded account (Finance Magnates, 18 September 2024). Single firms have given their own figures: 5% to 10% at The Funded Trader (its chief executive, 2025) and 25.07% at Hola Prime (its website, for October 2025 to September 2026).

What percentage of prop traders ever get a payout?

About 7% of traders received a payout in FPFX Tech's data on about 100,000 traders at 10 firms, and the average payout was 4% of the account size (Finance Magnates, 18 September 2024). At The Funded Trader, its chief executive said about 20% of those who passed were paid, which is about 1% to 2% of its clients.

Why do prop firms deny payouts?

In the 2027 10X Prop Trader Community Study (566 posts, 2 January to 26 November 2026), 94 posts reported a denied payout. The reason most often given was copy trading, account sharing or a shared IP or device (31 of 94), then overleveraging or "gambling" (18), then no reason or a vague one (13). Consistency rules came up as well: 2 posts said the firm refused a payout over one, and 6 more said one held a payout back.

Which prop firms have a static drawdown?

On a $100,000 2-step challenge, almost all of them. The 2027 10X Prop Firm Rules Index found that 20 of the 21 firms selling one use a static maximum loss, measured from your starting balance; only AquaFunded's is trailing (27 November 2026). Instant accounts are the other way round: 17 of the 21 use a trailing limit that follows your highest balance up, and only Audacity Capital, City Traders Imperium and Finotive Funding use a static one.

What is a consistency rule in a prop firm?

A consistency rule caps how much of your profit can come from one day. With a 15% rule, for example, no single day can make up more than 15% of your total profit before you can be paid. The 2027 10X Prop Firm Rules Index found one on the default set-up of 4 of the 21 $100,000 2-step challenges: Instant Funding (40%), AquaFunded (50%), Funded Trading Plus (35%) and FXIFY (25%), according to their own pages on 27 November 2026. Instant accounts use them far more: 14 of the 21 have one, most often at 15% or 20%.

How long do prop firm payouts take?

By the firms' own rules (27 November 2026), 14 of the 21 firms selling a 2-step allow a first payout 14 days after funding, and 3 allow one on demand. In the 2027 10X Prop Trader Community Study (566 posts, 2 January to 26 November 2026), 26 posts reported a late or missing payout. Of the 14 that said how long, the median wait was 14 days, with a range of 5 to 60.

How much do traders spend on prop firms?

In the 2027 10X Prop Trader Community Study (566 posts, 2 January to 26 November 2026), the 75 people who said what they'd spent on prop firms put it at $5,279 on average, leaving out the five biggest figures (over $40,000), and half had spent $2,000 or less. With all 80 included, the average was $10,072. A 2024 poll by PipFarm of 459 of its own clients found an average of $4,270.

How much does a prop firm challenge cost?

The median list price of a 2-step challenge, at firms that price in US dollars, was $199 at $25,000, $330 at $50,000, $549 at $100,000 and $1,087.50 at $200,000 (2027 10X Prop Firm Rules Index, 27 November 2026). Instant accounts cost more: a median $1,142 across the 17 priced in US dollars, at sizes from $20,000 to $100,000.

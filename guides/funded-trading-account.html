Funded trading accounts: 4 ways to trade bigger than your deposit (UK, 2027)

By 10X Trader Updated 23 November 2026 10 min read

A funded trading account is an account that lets you trade with more buying power than the money you put in. In the UK in 2027 there are four main routes: a prop firm challenge, an instant funding account, a first-loss account through a broker, or growing your own capital. They differ in three ways that matter: what you pay, whether the account is simulated or live, and what you can lose.

This guide compares all four side by side, shows how to check whether an account is real, and works through what each route costs, so you can pick the one that fits your account size and how you trade.

Key takeaways

  • There are four routes: a prop firm challenge, instant funding, a first-loss account or your own capital.
  • Most prop firm "funded" accounts are simulated; first-loss and own-capital accounts are live.
  • Fee-based routes cost the same whether you win or lose; live accounts cost you spread and commission on each trade.
  • A 10X first-loss account has no challenge or sign-up fee and you keep 100% of profit and losses. Leverage amplifies losses as well as gains. The most you can lose is your deposit.
  • Check any provider on the FCA register and warning list before you pay.

1. Prop firm challenge

How it works: you pay a fee to take an evaluation on a demo account. Hit the profit target without breaking the loss limits and you get a "funded" account. That account is usually simulated too, and the firm pays you a share of the profit you make on it.

Good for: traders with a tested strategy who are comfortable with strict rules and a deadline.

Watch out for: fees add up if you fail and retake. Rules such as daily loss limits, consistency rules and news-trading bans decide whether a payout is approved, so read them before you pay. Because most funded accounts are simulated, your payout depends on the firm's own terms, not on a live market account.

2. Instant funding

How it works: you pay a larger fee and skip the evaluation. You start trading a funded (usually simulated) account straight away, under a loss limit and payout rules.

Good for: traders who don't want an exam but are happy to pay for speed.

Watch out for: the fee is higher, the loss limits are often tighter, and profit shares can start lower and rise later.

3. First-loss account through a broker

How it works: this is how institutions fund traders, brought to a retail account. You put down a deposit, and that deposit is the "first loss": it takes any losses first. In return, the account gives you more buying power than the deposit.

With 10X, a $500 deposit trades with $5,000 of buying power: your $500 plus $4,500 of extra buying power from the partner broker under its account terms. After every closed trade, the buying power is reset to 10 times your new balance. A $100 win takes your balance to $600 and your buying power to $6,000; a $100 loss takes them to $400 and $4,000.

Good for: traders with a small account who want more buying power without paying challenge fees or passing a test.

Watch out for: you are trading your own money, so losses are real. Leverage amplifies losses as well as gains. The most you can lose is your deposit. You pay through the account's trading costs (spread and commission) rather than a fee, so compare the cost per trade, not just the headline. Accounts settle in US dollars, so a deposit in pounds is converted and a conversion fee may apply.

4. Growing your own capital

How it works: you trade your own account at a normal broker and add money over time.

Good for: long-term traders who want no outside rules at all and full control.

Watch out for: with a small balance, even a good month makes little money, which is why many traders look at the routes above. Under FCA rules (policy statement PS19/18), UK retail CFD accounts are limited to 30:1 leverage on major currency pairs, and retail clients can't lose more than the money in their CFD account (negative balance protection).

Simulated or live: how to tell

Most of the "funded accounts" advertised in the UK are simulated: your trades are not sent to the market, and the firm pays you from its own pocket under its terms. That isn't automatically bad, but it changes what you are buying. Four quick checks:

  1. Who holds the account? A live account sits with a named broker, and you can log in to it. If no broker is named, assume it's simulated.
  2. What is the payout called? Words like "reward", "performance fee" or "payout" (rather than your own account balance) usually mean a simulated account.
  3. Where does your money go? A fee is spent the moment you pay it. A deposit in a live account stays your balance until you trade it or withdraw it.
  4. What do the terms say about demo or simulated trading? Search the terms page for "simulated" and "demo". The terms are the place to check, not the homepage.

Checking a provider in the UK

Whichever route you choose:

  • Find who holds the money. For a live account, that's the broker. Look it up on the FCA register and check that the firm name and website match.
  • Search the FCA warning list for the firm's name and website before you pay anything.
  • Read the payout or withdrawal terms, not just the profit-share headline: minimum trading days, consistency rules, caps and processing times.
  • Search for recent reviews of payouts, not of sign-ups, and sort by newest.

How to choose

Ask yourself three questions:

  1. Do I want to pass a test? If yes, a prop firm challenge rewards proven discipline. If not, look at instant funding or a 10X broker account.
  2. Do I want a live account, or am I fine with simulated? Prop firms are mostly simulated; 10X broker accounts and own-capital accounts are live.
  3. What would I rather pay: fees or trading costs? Challenges and instant funding charge fees whether you win or lose. 10X broker accounts and own-capital accounts charge through spread and commission on each trade.

Questions traders ask

Is a funded trading account real money?

It depends on the route. Most prop firm funded accounts are simulated, and the firm pays you a share of the simulated profit. A first-loss account through a broker, like 10X, is a live account.

Can I get a funded trading account without a challenge?

Yes. Instant funding skips the challenge for a higher fee, and a first-loss account has no challenge, evaluation or sign-up fee. Prop firm challenges are still the most common route.

Are funded trading accounts legal in the UK?

Yes, UK traders can use them. What matters is checking who you're dealing with: look up the broker that holds a live account on the FCA register, and search the FCA warning list for any firm before you pay.

How do funded trading account providers make money?

Prop firms earn mainly from challenge and instant-funding fees. Brokers earn from trading costs. 10X earns through trading costs (spread and commission), not fees.

How much can I earn from a funded trading account?

There's no typical figure: it depends on your results, the account size and the payout rules. Profit shares range from about 80% to 100% at prop firms. With 10X you keep 100% of profit and losses.

What happens if I lose money on a funded trading account?

With a prop firm, the account is closed and you lose the fee. With a 10X first-loss account, losses come off your deposit, and the most you can lose is your deposit. With your own broker account, UK retail accounts have negative balance protection.

How long does it take to get a funded trading account?

A challenge takes as long as you need to hit its profit target, often a few weeks or more, plus time for checks. Instant funding and first-loss accounts can usually start once your identity checks and payment clear.

How much money do I need to start?

Prop firm challenges cost from a few pounds to several hundred, depending on account size. A 10X broker account starts from the deposit you choose.

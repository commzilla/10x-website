Funded trading account: 4 ways to trade bigger than your deposit (UK, 2027)

By 10X Trader Updated 23 November 2026 7 min read

A funded trading account lets you trade with more buying power than the money you put in. In 2027 there are four main routes: a prop firm challenge, an instant funding account, a first-loss account through a broker, or growing your own capital, and they differ in who owns the account, what you pay and what you can lose.

This guide compares all four, so you can pick the one that fits your account size and how you trade.

1. Prop firm challenge

How it works: you pay a fee to take an evaluation on a demo account. Hit the profit target without breaking the loss limits and you get a "funded" account, usually also simulated, where the firm pays you a share of the profit you make.

Good for: traders with a tested strategy who are comfortable with strict rules and a deadline.

Watch out for: fees add up if you fail and retake. Rules such as daily loss limits, consistency rules and news-trading bans decide whether a payout is approved, so read them before you pay. Many funded accounts are simulated, so your payout depends on the firm's own terms rather than on a live market account.

2. Instant funding

How it works: you pay a larger fee and skip the evaluation. You start trading a funded (usually simulated) account straight away, under a loss limit and payout rules.

Good for: traders who don't want an exam but are happy to pay for speed.

Watch out for: the fee is higher, the loss limits are often tighter, and profit shares can start lower and rise later.

3. First-loss account through a broker

How it works: this is how institutions fund traders, brought to a retail account. You put down a deposit, and that deposit is the "first loss": it takes any losses first. In return, the account gives you more buying power than the deposit.

With 10X, a $500 deposit trades with $5,000 of buying power: your $500 plus $4,500 of extra buying power from the partner broker under its account terms. After every closed trade, the buying power is reset to 10 times your new balance. A $100 win takes your balance to $600 and your buying power to $6,000; a $100 loss takes them to $400 and $4,000.

Good for: traders with a small account who want more buying power without paying challenge fees or passing a test.

Watch out for: you are trading your own money, so losses are real. The most you can lose is your deposit. Leverage makes losses bigger as well as gains. You pay through the account's trading costs (spread and commission) rather than a fee, so compare costs per trade, not just the headline. Accounts settle in US dollars, so a deposit in pounds is converted and a conversion fee may apply.

4. Growing your own capital

How it works: you trade your own account at a normal broker and add money over time.

Good for: long-term traders who want no outside rules at all and full control.

Watch out for: with a small balance, even a good month makes little money, which is why many traders look at the routes above. UK retail CFD accounts are limited to 30:1 leverage on major currency pairs under FCA rules.

How to choose

Ask yourself three questions:

  1. Do I want to pass a test? If yes, a prop firm challenge rewards proven discipline. If no, look at instant funding or a first-loss account.
  2. Do I want a live account or am I fine with simulated? Prop firms are mostly simulated; first-loss and own-capital accounts are live.
  3. What would I rather pay: fees or trading costs? Challenges and instant funding charge fees whether you win or lose. First-loss and own-capital accounts charge through spreads and commission on each trade.

Questions traders ask

Is a funded trading account real money?

It depends on the route. Most prop firm funded accounts are simulated, and the firm pays you a share of the simulated profit. A first-loss account through a broker, like 10X, is a live account.

Can I lose more than I put in?

With a prop firm, you risk the fee. With a 10X first-loss account, the most you can lose is your deposit. With your own broker account, UK retail accounts have negative balance protection.

Do I need to pass a challenge to get funded?

No. Instant funding and first-loss accounts skip the challenge. Prop firm challenges remain the most common route.

How much money do I need to start?

Prop firm challenges start from a fee of a few pounds to several hundred, depending on account size. A first-loss account starts from the deposit you choose.
